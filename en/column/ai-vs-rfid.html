Solving Cafeteria Checkout Staffing Shortages with "Unmanned Checkout" \u2014 AI vs. RFID Compared

What you'll learn in this article - How the two approaches that deliver "unmanned checkout" (AI image recognition and RFID) work, and their characteristics - An AI checkout vs. RFID checkout comparison table \u2014 checkout speed, accuracy, cost, and recommended facilities - The "hidden costs" you can cut beyond checkout labor - How to verify the effect with a free PoC

In this article, we thoroughly compare the two approaches that make unmanned checkout possible: AI image recognition checkout and RFID checkout. Please read to the end as material for deciding which method best suits your own cafeteria.

The two approaches to "unmanned checkout"

The means to fundamentally solve the labor shortage at cafeteria checkouts is "unmanned checkout." Currently, there are broadly two practical methods of unmanned checkout. We explain the mechanism and characteristics of each.

AI image recognition checkout \u2014 the camera automatically identifies the food

How it works: An AI camera (5-megapixel monocular color camera) mounted on the checkout counter photographs the food on the tray, and an image-recognition algorithm automatically determines the items and quantities. Based on the recognition results, it instantly calculates the amount.

Three key points:

\u2460 No special tableware needed (food-recognition mode) Because it can be introduced with ordinary tableware as-is, the cost of replacing tableware is zero. It can be introduced with almost no change to your existing cafeteria layout. Recognition accuracy is 99% or higher in food-recognition mode. If you choose the "tableware-recognition mode," which judges by the shape and color of the tableware, accuracy of 99.99% or higher can also be achieved. Note that in real cafeterias the number of menu items offered on a given day is limited to about 10\u201330, so even in food-recognition mode you can maintain nearly 100% accuracy in actual operation.

\u2461 Registering a menu item is just taking a photo with the camera To add a new menu item, the AI learns automatically just by photographing the food on the terminal. It can handle daily-changing menus on the same day, with registration completed in a few minutes during the morning prep.

\u2462 Keeping introduction costs down The only equipment required is the AI checkout counter. Because the AI engine is an edge-type design built into the checkout counter itself, no large-scale server installation is needed. Initial costs can be kept to a minimum.

Recommended for cafeterias like these: corporate cafeterias with a rich variety of daily menus, cafeterias with many items, facilities where replacing tableware is difficult

RFID checkout \u2014 instant reading with chip-embedded tableware

How it works: Menu information is recorded on an RFID chip embedded in the tableware. When the user places the tray on the RFID checkout counter, it reads the chips instantly and automatically calculates the total amount of multiple dishes.

Three key points:

\u2460 Fastest reading speed Even with about 30 dishes, it can recognize them all at once in one second, with recognition accuracy of nearly 100%. It shows its power in large-scale cafeterias that require mass processing. However, because RFID-embedded tableware has a maximum heat resistance of 85\u00b0C, caution is needed for high-temperature servings.

\u2461 Tableware can be reused as-is after washing RFID-embedded tableware is also compatible with commercial dishwashers. Because the information in the chip can be rewritten at any time, you can set different menus and prices for the same tableware in the morning, at noon, and in the evening.

\u2462 Batch rewriting supported Using a writing device (smart tableware information writer), writing menu information to the tableware is completed in a short time. The operational burden when changing menus can be minimized.

Recommended for cafeterias like these: large-scale cafeterias centered on fixed menus, facilities that need high-frequency use and mass processing

It's not just checkout labor costs you can cut

The benefits of introducing unmanned checkout are not limited to "reducing checkout staff labor costs." Because checkout data is automatically accumulated digitally, you can visualize and optimize every cost of cafeteria operation.

Automatic aggregation of management data

Sales data that used to be managed by hand or in Excel is now aggregated automatically in real time with each checkout. The information you can check from the PC management terminal is as follows.

Data item Content
Dashboard Check the day's sales, number of orders, and sales quantity by menu at a glance
Sales report Sales aggregation by day / week / month / meal occasion (breakfast/lunch/dinner)
Order management Detailed search of all orders, detection of and alerts for abnormal orders
Management analysis Cafeteria usage rate, average-spend trends, day-of-week trends

Because manual aggregation becomes unnecessary, the clerical burden on management staff is also greatly reduced.

Menu-level sales ranking

"Which menu is popular and which is left unsold" \u2014 because sales performance is visualized in real time, data-driven menu improvement becomes possible.

  • Popular menus: increase prep quantity to prevent lost opportunities
  • Unpopular menus: consider changing the seasoning or replacing them
  • Day-of-week trends: optimize prep based on trends such as "curry sells on Mondays" and "noodles are popular on Fridays"

By optimizing supplies (prep) that used to rely on experience and intuition based on sales data, you can also expect an effect of reducing food loss from overproduction by 15\u201330%.

Nutrition data analysis

By linking users' consumption data with nutritional content, you can provide personalized nutrition reports.

For companies that place importance on employee health management, cafeteria nutrition data can also become the foundation of health-focused management. The value of the welfare benefit of "protecting employees' health through company meals" becomes visible through data.
